# Lateral ankle sprain and chronic ankle instability (BASE Health Noosa guide library)

**Status: draft 1, 2 October 2026, written from published evidence for the clinic to review and edit.** Sources at the end with DOI/PMID (each checked against its Europe PMC record); tags like [T1] point to them. *(consensus)* marks expert agreement; *(practice)* an unvalidated clinical algorithm.

**Scope and use.** Exercise-based management of acute lateral ankle sprain and chronic ankle instability (CAI), including return-to-sport criteria, recurrence prevention and a note on syndesmosis ("high ankle") sprains. The physiotherapist diagnoses, grades and clears; Claude drafts the program for the stage given, using `rehab-principles.md` for the cross-cutting loading and pain rules and `training-variables.md` for general dosing.

---

## 1. Rules that matter most

1. **Treat it functionally.** Immobilise for less than 10 days and only for severe sprains; then a lace-up or semi-rigid brace plus exercise for 4–6 weeks beats bandage, tape or rest [T1]. Functional treatment improves swelling, motion, stiffness, instability and speed of return compared with immobilisation [T20].
2. **Start exercise as early as possible**; neuromuscular and proprioceptive training is a level-1 recommendation [T1] and early mobilisation has strong evidence for pain, swelling and function [T6].
3. **Exercise roughly halves recurrence in athletes**: exercise therapy RR 0.62 overall and 0.38 in athletes [T1]; the 2019 meta-analysis found exercise reduces reinjury but could not establish the content or parameters of an optimal program [T35], and the 2022 update of 14 trials (2,182 people) gives OR 0.60 at 12 months, though the effect weakens when high-risk trials are removed [T5]; an unsupervised 8-week home balance program cut recurrences from 33% to 22% (RR 0.63; NNT 9) [T32].
4. **Nobody knows the minimum dose.** Trials used 10–60 minute sessions (typically 30), 1–12 weeks, 5–84 sessions, and training volume did not change reinjury odds [T5]. A balance-led program of about 30 minutes, 2–3 sessions a week for 6–8 weeks is the typical trial dose, not a proven minimum [T5, T26, T27].
5. **Rehab content lags the way reinjury happens.** Reinjury is inversion plus internal rotation at ground contact with late peroneal activation during cutting, reactive play and high-speed landing, yet only 18% of trial exercises had a flight phase and 48% were sagittal-plane only [T16]. After the balance phase, train multiplanar landing, perturbation, cutting and reactive drills.
6. **For chronic instability combine balance and strength**: combined training SMD 1.28 for patient-reported function versus about 0.8 for either alone [T26]; neuromuscular plus strength ranked first for inversion muscle function [T27]; CAIT improves about 4.6 points and FAAM-ADL 7.7 with exercise (moderate certainty) [T25].
7. **Unstable-surface work transfers to reach tests, not to hops**: reach distances improve while anterior reach, side-hop and figure-of-8 hop do not, so hopping, landing and cutting must be trained directly [T28].
8. **Return to sport has domains but no validated cut-offs.** The PAASS consensus (155 experts) requires pain, ankle impairments (ROM, strength, endurance, power), athlete perception (confidence, stability, readiness), sensorimotor control and sport performance (hopping, jumping, agility, sport drills, a full training session) [T3]; no study has validated a criterion [T8] and only 2 of 11 expert papers give a threshold (≥80% of the other limb) [T18]. An Ankle-GO score under 8/25 carried a 9-fold recurrence risk in one 54-patient cohort [T9].
9. **Early clearance is the norm and the problem**: 71–75% of US high-school athletes are cleared within 3 days and 95% within 10 [T3]; a third of a return-to-sport cohort reinjured within 2 years [T9]; up to 40% of first sprains develop chronic instability [T7].
10. **Prevent the next one**: bracing or taping cuts recurrence (RR 0.30) and first sprains (RR 0.69) [T1]; neuromuscular training RR 0.60 overall and 0.69 in those with a previous sprain [T12]; bracing and balance training have no clear winner head to head [T14, T15]. Tape loses most of its restriction within about 20 minutes; braces need re-tightening about every 45 minutes [T13].
11. **Screen for fracture and for the syndesmosis**: Ottawa rules (sensitivity 86–99%) [T1]; a positive squeeze or external-rotation test, anterior syndesmosis tenderness or injury to the ATFL plus deltoid point to a high ankle sprain, which returns in about 45 days when stable and 64 when unstable, and often needs fixation when unstable [T22, T23, T33].
12. **Ice and NSAIDs are a judgement call**: strong evidence for short-term NSAIDs with early mobilisation [T6] versus guideline caution that NSAIDs may delay healing (paracetamol equal for pain) [T1] and the PEACE & LOVE position against anti-inflammatories [T10].

## 2. Classification and assessment that change the plan

- **Guidelines**: the 2018 BJSM guideline [T1] and the 2021 JOSPT clinical practice guideline (first-time sprain and CAI) [T2] are the two current references; the JOSPT text could not be read in this pass, so its grades are not quoted here.
- **Diagnosis**: delayed physical examination at 4–5 days is most accurate (anterior drawer sensitivity 84%, specificity 96%) [T1]; the ROAST consensus sets a minimum standard (mechanism, bones, ligaments) and a second, rehabilitation-oriented assessment of the mechanical and sensorimotor impairments that lead to CAI [T4].
- **Grade and expected course**: grade I about 2 weeks; grade II–III 3–6 weeks of functional restriction and 6–8 weeks to full return; after surgery 12–16 weeks [T1]; syndesmotic injury averages 46 days [T20].
- **Chronic ankle instability** (International Ankle Consortium criteria): a significant sprain ≥12 months ago with ≥1 day lost, the latest injury >3 months ago, ≥2 giving-way episodes in 6 months or recurrent sprain or perceived instability, plus a questionnaire threshold (CAIT ≤24, IdFAI ≥11 or AII ≥5 "yes") [T19]. The updated model frames CAI as interacting pathomechanical, sensory-perceptual and motor-behavioural impairments shaped by beliefs and context [T7]; soccer has the highest recurrence (61%) and track and field the most perceived instability (41%) [T30].
- **Poor-prognosis markers**: unable to jump and land by 2 weeks, dynamic postural-control deficit, altered hip kinematics, greater laxity at 8 weeks, high-level sport, young male, higher BMI [T1].

## 3. Stages and criteria

| Stage | Goal | Content | Progress when | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acute (days 0–10) | Protect, settle, move | Brace (not cast) unless severe; weight-bearing as tolerated; active ROM; isometrics; PEACE & LOVE | Walking without limp; swelling stable; pain-free ankle ROM in sagittal plane | ≤10 days [T1, T10, T20] |
| Restore (weeks 1–6) | ROM, strength, balance | Evertor/invertor and calf strengthening (bands → weighted heel rises), hip abductors; balance progressions double → single leg, eyes open → closed, stable → unstable, with perturbations; manual mobilisation for dorsiflexion if limited | Dorsiflexion within ~10° of the other side (weight-bearing lunge test) [T34]; single-leg balance steady with eyes closed; symmetric heel-rise endurance | 4–6 weeks of functional treatment [T1, T14] |
| Load (weeks 3–8) | Landing, hopping, running | Bilateral → unilateral hops and landings, multiplanar, then flight-phase and reactive tasks; linear running → bounds → speed | Side-hop, figure-of-8 and single-leg hop symmetric (~90%); running pain-free | [T16, T24, T28] |
| Sport (weeks 6–8+) | Cutting, reactive play, full training | Change of direction, perturbation and reactive drills, sport-specific tasks, a full training session with brace or tape | PAASS domains met; Ankle-GO ≥8/25; athlete confident | grade II–III 6–8 weeks [T1, T3, T9] |
| Prevention (ongoing) | Keep it | Brace or tape in sport for at least a season; balance program 2–3×/week | — | [T1, T12, T32] |

## 4. Exercise selection by stage

- **Balance and neuromuscular**: single-leg stance progressions, wobble board or foam, eyes closed, perturbations; balance training works with or without devices [T12, T14]; the Hupperets program was unsupervised, home-based, 8 weeks, three 30-minute sessions a week after usual care [T32].
- **Strength**: evertors and invertors (bands), plantarflexors (heel rises to the clinic's norms, see `muscle-strains.md`), hip abductors; combine with balance for the best patient-reported function in CAI [T26, T27].
- **Mobility**: joint mobilisation adds short-term dorsiflexion gain to exercise [T1]; measure with the weight-bearing lunge test (reliable; MDC around 1–2 cm / 4–5°) [T34].
- **Hop, land, cut**: anterior-reach, side-hop and figure-of-8 performance need specific practice; add flight-phase, multiplanar and reactive tasks, because trial programs omit them and reinjury happens there [T16, T28].
- **Football algorithm** *(practice, unvalidated)*: four levels — proprioception plus closed-chain strength and bike; linear running and bounds; jump abilities, ladders and reactive drills; multidirectional sprint and sport-specific work — gated by an Ankle Function Score (>40, >60, >75, >85, >95 for return), ROM within 10°, swelling stable and limb symmetry ≥90% on Stork/Y-balance, heel-rise and running, side-hop and triple hop, then square, crossover and 6 m timed hop [T24].

## 5. Dosage

| Program | Dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute exercise | Start as early as possible; neuromuscular and proprioceptive content; functional support 4–6 weeks | T1 |
| Reinjury-prevention trials | Balance-led, ~30 min (10–60), 5–84 sessions, 1–12 weeks; median 12 h total; volume did not change outcome | T5 |
| Home balance (Hupperets) | 8 weeks, 3 × 30 min/week, unsupervised, after usual care; recurrence 22% vs 33% | T32 |
| CAI exercise (subgroups, low certainty) | 6-week programs, >3 sessions/week, >30 min per session gave the best function in one meta-analysis [T26]; multimodal training 1–2×/week for 5–8 weeks in another [T25]; typical trials 3×/week, 20–60 min, 6–8 weeks [T27]; unstable-surface training 4–6 weeks, 2–3×/week [T28] | T25–T28 |
| Strength doses | Not given by any accessible guideline; use `training-variables.md` (strength 3–4 × 6–12; endurance 2–3 × 15–25) | — |
| Prevention | Brace or tape during sport for at least one season after a sprain; balance training 2–3×/week; team neuromuscular programs | T1, T12, T14 |

## 6. Pain and load rules

- Protect for 1–3 days with pain as the guide, then load progressively within symptoms (PEACE & LOVE) [T10]; early weight-bearing within pain tolerance is part of functional treatment [T1].
- Swelling that increases after a session means the dose was too high; the football algorithm allows no more than a 1% increase in girth between tests *(practice)* [T24].
- Hopping, cutting and sport tasks pain-free before clearance; pain during sport and over the last 24 hours is the first PAASS domain [T3].
- Medication: paracetamol equals NSAIDs for pain, swelling and ROM; NSAIDs only short term (<14 days) with caution [T1]; ice helps swelling only with exercise [T1, T20].

## 7. Return to sport

- Domains *(consensus, 155 experts, 98% agreement)*: pain; ankle impairments (ROM, strength, endurance, power); athlete perception (confidence, perceived stability, psychological readiness); sensorimotor control (proprioception, dynamic balance); sport/functional performance (hopping, jumping, agility, sport-specific drills, a complete training session) [T3].
- Tests in use: Star Excursion / Y-balance symmetry, single-leg hop, side-hop, figure-of-8 hop, triple and crossover hops, heel-rise endurance, weight-bearing lunge, CAIT/FAAM/IdFAI [T8, T18, T24]. Thresholds are expert opinion: ≥80% of the other limb in two papers [T18], ≥90% in the football algorithm [T24].
- Ankle-GO (two questionnaires plus four functional tests, 25 points): a score below 8 at return gave OR 8.6 for recurrence within 2 years (AUC 0.75); 33% of the cohort reinjured [T9].
- Timing: grade I ~2 weeks, grade II–III 6–8 weeks to full sport, surgery 12–16 weeks [T1]; syndesmosis stable ~45 days, unstable ~64 days (surgical) [T33]; elite athletes after early lateral-ligament repair for grade III returned at a median 69 days with 2% reinjury (uncontrolled) [T31].
- Outcome measures: CAIT (0–30, lower = more unstable; ≤24 = instability), IdFAI ≥11, AII ≥5 [T19]; exercise improves CAIT by ~4.6 and FAAM-ADL by ~7.7 points [T25]. MCIDs for CAIT and FAAM were not retrieved.

## 8. Prevention

- External support: brace or tape, RR 0.30 for recurrence and 0.69 for first sprains; choose by preference; no proprioceptive benefit [T1]; lace-up braces HR 0.30–0.39 in basketball; NNT 18 for braces vs 39–57 for tape per season; small performance costs (vertical jump up to −5%, sprint −3.5%) [T13].
- Neuromuscular/balance training: RR 0.60 overall, 0.58 balance-only, 0.69 with a previous sprain; first-time prevention evidence insufficient; compliance and dose–response unexamined [T12]. Bracing RR 0.36 vs balance training RR 0.54 in athletes [T14]; head to head no pooled difference, with the largest trial favouring the brace [T15].
- Lower body weight and BMI and no mechanical defect lower CAI risk [T21].
- Avoid: immobilisation ≥10 days, kinesiotape or elastic bandage instead of a brace, electrotherapy or ultrasound for acute sprain, routine surgery (60–70% respond to conservative care; surgery slightly less long-term pain but 5 times the complications) [T1, T6, T20].

## 9. Red lines

- Ottawa-positive (bone tenderness at the malleoli, base of the fifth metatarsal or navicular; unable to bear weight four steps) → imaging [T1].
- Positive squeeze or external-rotation test, anterior syndesmosis tenderness, or symptoms persisting beyond 6 weeks → imaging and referral; unstable syndesmosis usually needs fixation [T23, T33].
- No clearance within days on symptoms alone; a full training session and the PAASS domains come first [T3].
- Do not progress to cutting and reactive drills before hop and landing tasks are symmetric and pain-free [T16, T28].

## 10. Evidence confidence and gaps

- Strong: functional treatment over immobilisation; exercise reduces recurrence (fragile in sensitivity analyses); bracing and neuromuscular programs prevent recurrence; combined balance and strength for CAI.
- Moderate: balance training dose (no dose–response); unstable-surface specificity; Ankle-GO prognostic value (one cohort).
- Weak or missing: the 2021 JOSPT guideline's specific grades and doses (record verified, text not read); strength sets/reps/loads; lunge-test targets; CAIT/FAAM MCIDs; residual-symptom and osteoarthritis rates; compliance data; any validated return-to-sport threshold.
- Labels corrected from the first pass: the exercise-rehabilitation update is in PLOS ONE [T5]; the CAI selection criteria are Gribble 2013, while the 2016 BJSM paper is the prevalence and consequences review [T19, T29].
