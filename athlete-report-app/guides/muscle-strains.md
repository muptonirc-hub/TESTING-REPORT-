# Muscle strain injuries: hamstring in depth, calf, adductor and quadriceps (BASE Health Noosa guide library)

**Status: draft 2, 2 October 2026, written from published evidence for the clinic to review and edit.** Sources at the end with DOI/PMID (each checked against its Europe PMC record); tags like [T4] point to them. *(consensus)* marks expert agreement, *(practice)* a published clinical programme rather than a trial, *(to verify)* a paper not opened in this pass.

**Scope and use.** Exercise programming after an acute muscle strain: how classification changes the plan, what to load when, the pain rule, the running and sprint build, the criteria for moving on and for return to play, and the prevention dose that follows. It applies to the Hamstring rehab tool (phases: Initial assessment, Week 1, Week 2–6, Return to play) and to calf, adductor and quadriceps strains entered as a condition. The physiotherapist sets the grade, the phase and the return decision; Claude drafts the program for the phase given. General loading and pain rules are in `rehab-principles.md`; dosing by adaptation in `training-variables.md`.

---

## 1. Rules that matter most

1. **Tendon involvement is the one imaging feature that changes the plan.** Intramuscular-tendon ("c") hamstring injuries, proximal free-tendon (stretch-type) injuries, calf injuries with aponeurosis failure and grade 3 adductor injuries return later and recur more; stage them separately with a longer protected phase [T3, T9, T10, T13–T15, T22, T27]. Keep the effect in proportion: in 70 footballers, full-thickness intramuscular-tendon disruption added only about a week (22 days without involvement) with wide overlap [T25].
2. **Grade explains little of the return time otherwise** (7.6–11.9% of the variance; 17–31% of clinical hamstring injuries are MRI-negative), so progress by criteria, not by a date [T1, T3]. Classification systems in use: BAMIC grades 0–4 with a/b/c for myofascial, musculotendinous and intratendinous sites [T20]; Munich types 1–4 (functional vs structural) [T21]; MLG-R (mechanism, location, grade, re-injuries) [T23].
3. **Pain up to 4/10 during strengthening did not slow return or raise reinjury** (15 vs 17 days; strength about 15% better; fascicle length +0.9 cm) in a 43-man RCT [T4]; sprinting must be pain-free before return *(consensus 97%)* [T1].
4. **Train both hamstring actions**: hip-extension-dominant work (RDL, 45° hip extension, bridges) loads biceps femoris long head and semimembranosus; knee-flexion-dominant work (Nordic, sliders, curls) loads semitendinosus; both are needed and the end-goal is long-length eccentric and isometric strength *(consensus 95%)* [T1, T11].
5. **Lengthening (long-muscle-length) exercise is the best-tested content.** Askling's L-protocol (the extender, the diver and the glider) returned footballers in 28 vs 51 days and sprinters/jumpers in 49 vs 86 days against conventional exercises, with fewer reinjuries [T26, T27]. Eccentric loading starts in week 1 in low-grade injuries [T4, T5] and is delayed about 3 weeks in tendon injuries, where isometrics above 80% MVC lead *(practice)* [T9].
6. **A criteria-based algorithm cut reinjury from 25% to 4% without slowing return** (n=48) [T5]; all nine protocols in the systematic review progressed by pain and none had validated criteria [T7].
7. **Running is rehab, not a test at the end**: on-field running in every program (98%), built to full sprinting (100%) at game-level volume and intensity (95%), benchmarked with GPS where available (83%) *(consensus)* [T1].
8. **A 10% strength-symmetry rule is not endorsed** (66% agreement) and a hand-held dynamometer cannot detect it (minimal detectable difference 24–48%); 67% of footballers had a >10% isokinetic deficit at clearance anyway [T1, T16, T17]. Use symmetry as a flag, add absolute values and sprint exposure.
9. **Return-to-play criteria that have consensus**: pain-free sprinting, a full unrestricted training session, the athlete's own readiness, shared decision-making; MRI does not decide return [T1, T6].
10. **Recurrences cluster early**: within 2 months of return in football and in the first days after clearance in the trials, so the first month back needs a maintenance dose and conservative load [T4, T8, T16].
11. **Nordic programmes roughly halve hamstring injuries** (RR 0.49 across 8,459 athletes; IRR 0.49 in football) when compliance is high [T28, T29]; short biceps femoris fascicles (<10.56 cm, RR 4.1) and low eccentric strength (<337 N, RR 4.4) are the modifiable risks they target [T30]; fascicles lengthen within 6 weeks (+23% with high volume) and shorten back within 2 weeks of stopping, while strength holds for at least 4 weeks, so keep a weekly maintenance dose [T12, T31].
12. **Calf and adductor return times are predictable from tissue involvement**: calf MRI grade 0–3 returns at 8, 17, 25 and 48 days; adductor grade 0–2 at about 18 days and grade 3 at about 78 days, with reinjury 3% when the pain-free milestone was met versus 21% when it was not [T14, T15].

## 2. Classification that changes exercise choice

- The London consensus wants one classification with agreed terms; in practice BAMIC is primary for 58% of experts, Munich 12%, MLG-R 6%; name the muscle (89%), separate free-tendon from intramuscular-tendon involvement (87%), pair imaging with functional criteria (90%) [T3]. BAMIC: grades 0–4 by MRI extent, suffix a (myofascial), b (musculotendinous junction) or c (intratendinous) [T20]. Munich: functional disorders (types 1–2, no fibre tear) vs structural injuries (type 3 partial, type 4 (sub)total tear or avulsion) [T21]. MLG-R: mechanism, location, grade and number of re-injuries [T23].
- **Hamstring, British Athletics classes** *(practice)*: a (myofascial) acute 0–2 days, remodelling 2–5, functional 5–10; b (musculotendinous junction) 0–5, 5–14, 14–28 days; c (intratendinous) protected weeks 0–2, remodelling 2–6, functional 6–9 (3c longer) [T9]. Pollock 2016 (65 injuries, elite track and field): time to full training rose with grade and "c" injuries had significantly more recurrence [T10].
- **Mechanism**: stretch-type injuries (proximal free tendon, semimembranosus) take far longer than sprint-type injuries: 43 vs 23 days in footballers and 73 vs 31 days in sprinters even with the better protocol [T26, T27]. Intramuscular tendon disruption was present in 41% of 70 acute injuries and lengthened return by about a week only when full-thickness with tendon waviness [T25]. Record the mechanism (82% agreement) [T3].
- **Calf**: soleus 69%, medial gastrocnemius 27%; MRI grades 0–3 by connective-tissue (aponeurosis) involvement; aponeurotic disruption and a running mechanism lengthen return [T13, T14].
- **Adductor**: MRI grade 0–2 versus grade 3 (complete/large) is the split that matters [T15].
- **Quadriceps**: central (intramuscular) tendon injuries of rectus femoris, like those of biceps femoris, need prolonged rehabilitation and recur more [T22]; no rehabilitation trial was found, so apply the tendon rule and the general staging.
- Typical team-sport hamstring injuries are low grade: median clearance 15–17 days when diagnosed clinically [T4]. In men's professional football 22% of players sustain a hamstring injury each season, match rates are 9 times training rates, and hamstring injuries rose from 12% to 24% of all injuries between 2001 and 2022 [T32, T33]. Older age (SMD 1.6) and any previous hamstring injury (RR 2.7) are the strongest risk factors across 71,324 athletes [T34].

## 3. Stages and criteria (hamstring; adapt by tissue for the others)

| Stage (app phase) | Goal | Entry/exit criteria | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acute / protect (Initial assessment, Week 1) | Settle pain, keep moving, start isometrics and pain-limited loading | Pain-free walking; palpation and active knee-extension deficit recorded; exit when daily tasks are pain-free and bilateral exercises are tolerated at ≤4/10 | a: 0–2 d; b: 0–5 d; c: 0–2 weeks (protected) [T4, T9] |
| Remodelling / load (Week 2–6) | Rebuild long-length strength, eccentric capacity, trunk and pelvis control; begin running | Progress when the prescribed reps are completed through full range within the pain limit; add load in ~5 kg steps [T4]; running starts pain-free and builds through 9 stages [T4] | b: weeks 1–4; c: weeks 2–6 [T9] |
| Functional / sport (Week 2–6 → Return to play) | Sprint exposure to game intensity, agility, sport skills, full training | Pain-free running before sprinting (86%); 3-day blocks of sprint / strength / trunk, at least 3 blocks [T5]; GPS volumes approach match loads [T1] | b: weeks 3–5; c: weeks 6–9 [T9] |
| Return to play | Clearance and the first month back | Pain-free palpation; active knee extension ≥90% of other side; pain-free maximal isometrics at 0°/0° and 90°/90°; no pain or apprehension at 100% perceived sprint; full unrestricted training; athlete ready; shared decision [T1, T4, T6] | Low grade 15–26 d; c 6–9+ weeks [T4, T5, T9] |

Tool metrics that map to these criteria: AKET/PKET deficit and SLR % (flexibility within 0–10% of the other side reached consensus in the FIFA Delphi [T6]); HHD knee-flexion force at 90° and 15° (use for pain-free maximal isometrics; the 10% rule is inside the measurement error [T17]); Nordic peak force and imbalance (British Athletics returns class b/c at pre-injury eccentric strength *(practice)* [T9]); 5 m and 10 m sprint times (test sprinting directly: strength tests explained <9% of sprint variance in one cohort [T19]); HaOS and pain on palpation.

## 4. Exercise selection by stage

- **Acute**: isometric knee-flexion holds (inner to mid range, building to >80% MVC in tendon injuries), bridges (double then single leg), isometric curls, Roman-chair isometrics; avoid long-length and high-velocity loads in week 1 *(practice/consensus)* [T1, T9].
- **Remodelling**: the lengthening exercises of the L-protocol — the extender (hip flexed, slow knee extension), the diver (single-leg hip hinge with arm reach) and the glider (slide into a split with the injured leg behind) — are the best-tested content [T26, T27]; the Hickey/Mendiguchia set is the other trial-tested core: hamstring bridge (10–12 → 8–10 reps), 45° hip extension (8–10 → 6–8), eccentric slider (6–8 → 4–6), Nordic (4–6), bilateral then unilateral, 2 supervised sessions/week [T4]; add RDL and single-leg RDL (hip-dominant), hamstring curls (knee-dominant), lumbopelvic control in three planes (leg lowers, single-leg squat, Pallof press) and long-duration single-leg Roman-chair isometrics *(practice)* [T9]. Both hip- and knee-dominant exercises each session; no agreed order [T1].
- **Functional**: sprint technique and accelerations (6–8 × 20–50 m per sprint day), strength day (eccentric, hip- and knee-dominant, plyometrics), trunk/mobility day, in repeating 3-day blocks [T5]; heavy flywheel or loaded Nordic late *(practice)* [T9].
- **Calf**: stage by soleus (bent-knee) versus gastrocnemius (straight-knee) and by aponeurosis involvement; progress seated and standing calf raises from isometric to heavy slow to fast, then hopping and running [T13, T14]. The standing heel-rise test is highly reliable (ICC 1.0; between-day difference ±6 reps); healthy adults manage a median 24 (men) and 21 (women) repetitions, more when younger and more active [T35]. Age and a previous calf strain are the strongest risk factors for calf injury [T36].
- **Adductor**: adductor injuries are 66% of acute groin injuries (adductor longus in >90%), iliopsoas and proximal rectus femoris 15–25%, and 22% have no MRI finding [T37]. The Serner programme: nine standardised exercises including the Copenhagen adduction exercise, 3×/week on alternate days, each to volitional failure at pain ≤2/10, then running and sport-specific work [T15]. The Copenhagen exercise alone, 8 weeks in season with 91% compliance and soreness 0–2/10, produced a large eccentric adduction strength gain [T38].
- **Quadriceps**: isometric and short-range knee-extension loading first, then lengthened (hip-extended) loading, then kicking and sprinting; evidence not verified in this pass.

Pain rule for the whole table: strengthening to ≤4/10 that settles by the next session [T4]; running build-up may carry mild pain if individualised (84%) but sprinting is pain-free (97%) [T1].

## 6. Running and sprint build

- Trial ladder: 9 stages from walking to sprinting over 50 m, advance after 3 repetitions at a stage within the pain limit [T4].
- British Athletics *(practice, elite sprinters)*: class b drills week 1, ~60–70% max velocity week 2 (10 × 50 m), 70–80% week 3, 80–90% week 4 with bends, >90% and blocks week 5; class c drills weeks 1–2, jogging week 3, 10 × 50–100 m weeks 4–5, ~80% week 6, 80–90% week 7, >90% weeks 8–9; class a pool running day 1–2, drills, 5 × 100 m, near race pace by day 6–7 [T9].
- Criteria-based algorithm: sprint day = 6–8 × 20–50 m accelerations/sprints, repeated every third day, at least 3 blocks before return; top speed 8.4 vs 7.7 m/s favoured the algorithm group [T5].
- Return requires game-level running volume and intensity and pain-free maximal sprinting; GPS benchmarks against the athlete's own match data [T1]. Adductor return gate: 10 × 30 m pain-free sprints plus agility tests (T-test, Illinois) [T15].

## 7. Return to play: criteria and timing

- Published football batteries use a median of 6 criteria (range 4–8), most often absence of pain, ≥90% strength symmetry and functional tests, with psychological readiness and GPS appearing only after 2020 [T2].
- Consensus criteria: full unrestricted training session (93%), pain-free sprinting (97%), athlete confidence and self-assessment (98%, 87%), sport-specific endurance (78%), peak isometric and eccentric strength considered (83%) without a fixed threshold, GPS match-load benchmark (83%); flexibility rejected as a criterion (45%); H-test not endorsed (58%); MRI not used [T1]. FIFA Delphi: no pain on palpation, strength, flexibility or functional testing; flexibility within 0–10%; GPS match simulation (82%), repeated-sprint test (76%), single-leg bridge (71%), deceleration drills (71%); mentally ready; shared decision [T6].
- Practice batteries: pain-free palpation, SLR/active knee extension ≥90%, pain-free maximal isometrics 0°/0° and 90°/90°, 100% perceived sprint without pain or apprehension [T4]; British Athletics adds Nordbord eccentric strength at pre-injury level, single-leg bridge >30 (unit to verify), force-plate and stride comparisons for class c [T9]. The Askling H-test (active ballistic straight-leg raise with an insecurity rating) is reliable (ICC 0.94–0.99) and detects the apprehension that the standard examination misses [T39], though the London consensus did not endorse it as a criterion [T1].
- Times: clinically diagnosed hamstring 15–17 days [T4]; semi-professional football 23–26 days [T5]; each BAMIC step adds ~3–4 days, being helped off the pitch adds ~11 [T16]; class c 6–9+ weeks [T9]; calf 8–48 days by MRI grade [T14]; adductor 18 vs 78 days [T15].
- Reinjury: 4% (criteria algorithm) vs 25% (lengthening protocol) [T5]; ~11% at 6 months in the pain-rule trial, all within 26 days of injury [T4]; about 18% in professional football, mostly within 2 months [T16]; adductor 8% at 12 months [T15]; calf recurrence predicted by prior calf strain (HR 6.7), prior ankle injury (3.9) and age [T13].

## 8. Prevention after return

- Nordic hamstring programmes: RR 0.49 (0.32–0.74) across 15 studies and 8,459 athletes, 0.52 in randomised trials alone [T28]; IRR 0.49 in football [T29]; benefit tracks compliance (~91% effective, ~21% not); 2–3 × 6–12, 1–3 sessions/week, 4–10 weeks; ~48 reps/week for architectural change, then maintenance [T12]. Fascicle gains revert within 2 weeks of stopping [T31]. Only 44% of players who know the exercise use it [T18].
- Keep sprint exposure in the weekly plan (consensus places game-intensity sprinting in rehab and training) [T1]; Copenhagen adduction programme for groin: see `groin-pain.md`.
- Eccentric strength and fascicle length respond within the rehab period itself (+1.7–2.0 cm fascicle length by clearance) [T4]; the targets are eccentric knee-flexor strength above ~337 N and biceps femoris long-head fascicles above ~10.6 cm, below which risk is 4-fold [T30].

## 9. Red lines

- Suspected complete tear or proximal avulsion (palpable defect, large active knee-extension deficit, severe difficulty walking) and any neurological or vascular sign: medical review [see `rehab-principles.md`].
- No sprinting, kicking or return with pain or apprehension [T1, T4].
- No eccentric overload or long-length loading in the first weeks of a tendon ("c") injury *(practice)* [T9].
- MRI grade is not a return criterion [T1, T6]; a hand-held dynamometer side-to-side difference under ~25% is within error [T17].
- Static stretching is low priority and may impair elastic function *(consensus)* [T1]. Platelet-rich plasma does not speed return (no benefit over rehabilitation alone in 90 athletes; no benefit in the Dutch multicentre trial) [T40, T41]; NSAIDs and corticosteroids have no consensus; the adductor trial excluded them [T1, T15].

## 10. Evidence confidence and gaps

- Moderate: pain-threshold rehab (one RCT, n=43, men) [T4]; criteria-based algorithm (one RCT, n=48, men) [T5]; consensus criteria (two Delphi studies) [T1, T6]; calf and adductor return times (cohorts) [T13–T15].
- Weak: exact dosage tables (British Athletics practice, single-athlete examples) [T9]; Nordic dose (umbrella of low-rated reviews) [T12]; everything on quadriceps strains.
- Not verified in this pass: Whiteley 2018 (daily examination of 131 injuries and running speed); Edouard 2019 sprint-exposure paper (not indexed); Harøy 2019 is cited in `rehab-principles.md` and `groin-pain.md`.
- Women, adolescents and recreational athletes are absent from the trials.
