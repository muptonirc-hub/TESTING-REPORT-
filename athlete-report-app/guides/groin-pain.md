# Groin pain in athletes: adductor-, iliopsoas-, inguinal-, pubic- and hip-related (BASE Health Noosa guide library)

**Status: draft 1, 2 October 2026, written from published evidence for the clinic to review and edit.** Sources at the end with DOI/PMID (each checked against its Europe PMC record); tags like [T1] point to them. *(consensus)* marks expert agreement; *(inference)* a working rule that no study tested.

**Scope and use.** Exercise-based management of long-standing groin pain in athletes under the Doha classification, and of hip-related pain (femoroacetabular impingement syndrome) in young and middle-aged active adults; acute adductor strains are covered in `muscle-strains.md`. The physiotherapist makes the entity diagnosis and screens the hip; Claude drafts the program for the entity and stage given, using `rehab-principles.md` and `training-variables.md` for the cross-cutting rules and dosing.

---

## 1. Rules that matter most

1. **Classify by examination, then treat the entity.** Doha: adductor-, iliopsoas-, inguinal- and pubic-related groin pain; hip-related groin pain; other causes [T1]. Adductor-related pain is 68% of time-loss groin injuries in men's professional football, iliopsoas 12%, pubic 9%; median time loss 10 days and 21% of players are affected each season [T16]. Three adductor tests (resisted outer-range adduction, long-lever squeeze, passive stretch) predict an adductor lesion with 80–81% probability, while hip-flexor tests predict poorly [T22].
2. **Active training beats passive treatment**: for long-standing adductor-related pain, an 8–12-week strength and coordination program returned 23 of 34 athletes to sport pain-free versus 4 of 34 with passive physiotherapy (OR 12.7) [T23]; passive modalities add nothing to exercise (very low certainty) [T15].
3. **A criterion-based, whole-chain program works across entities**: intersegmental control and compound strength → linear running → multidirectional and reactive work returned 73% of 205 athletes pain-free at a mean 9.9 weeks after about 5 visits, with no difference by diagnosis, and without isolated adductor exercises [T3].
4. **Adductor strength and the squeeze test are the monitoring tools**: athletes with groin pain have pain and lower force on the squeeze test, reduced hip internal rotation and bent-knee fall-out, altered trunk muscle function and lower PROMs [T24]; discharge in King's program required a pain-free squeeze at 0°, 45° and 90° (94% met it) [T3]. Use them to progress and discharge, not to predict *(inference)*.
5. **The Copenhagen adduction exercise is the prevention dose with a trial behind it**: one set per side, 2–3 times a week building from 3–5 to 12–15 reps over 8 pre-season weeks, then once a week in season, cut the odds of groin problems by 41% (OR 0.59) in 652 footballers [T9]; eccentric adduction strength rises by about 0.6 Nm/kg (+36% in one trial) after 8 weeks [T10, T11, T25]. Step down a level if pain exceeds 3/10 [T9].
6. **Hip-related pain is a different problem.** FAI syndrome needs symptoms, signs and imaging together [T4]; exclude serious and competing causes (stress fracture, SCFE, tumour, lumbar spine) [T21]; physiotherapist-led treatment should be exercise-based, hip plus trunk plus functional strengthening, for at least 3 months, with PROMs and shared decision-making *(consensus)* [T2].
7. **For FAI syndrome, arthroscopy beat short physiotherapy at 8–12 months** (iHOT-33 +6.8, just above the 6.1 MCID, in FASHIoN; HOS-ADL +10 in FAIT; pooled iHOT-33 +11 to +13) [T5, T6, T7, T17], but the physiotherapy arms were 6–12 sessions over 6 weeks to 5 months, short of the ≥3 months of progressive strengthening the consensus recommends [T2]; no placebo-controlled trial of physiotherapy exists and effects range from moderate (vs no treatment) to weakly negative (vs arthroscopy) [T26].
8. **Imaging over-reads in footballers**: up to 71% of asymptomatic players show pubic bone-marrow oedema, adductor tendinopathy or degenerative change (OR 6–13 vs non-players), so MRI findings must be matched to the clinical entity [T27]; 21% of acute groin injuries are MRI-negative [T22].
9. **Rehab dosing is poorly reported**: only 61% of hip-related-pain exercise studies gave sets, reps and frequency and 6 anchored load to RPE or %RM [T8]; King's program reports none [T3]. Numeric doses in this guide beyond the Copenhagen trials come from `training-variables.md`.
10. **Expect about 10 weeks** for a criterion-based program in chronic cases (median symptom duration 32 weeks at entry) [T3]; Hölmich's program ran 8–12 weeks [T23]; Zurich asks for ≥3 months in hip-related pain [T2]. No clear progress by about 12 weeks is the point to re-examine the entity, re-screen the hip (FADIR, ROM) and discuss referral *(inference)*.
11. **Prevention is multifactorial**: Copenhagen alone has a limited effect on substantial problems (OR 0.82, NS) [T9, T13]; combine it with global neuromuscular training and load management [T20]; a 2025 Copenhagen meta-analysis is retracted and must not be cited [T11].
12. **Women differ**: in elite female team-sport athletes iliopsoas-related pain was the commonest entity (59% of cases) and strength did not relate to function [T19]; female national-team norms give eccentric adduction 2.3–2.5 Nm/kg and an adduction:abduction ratio near 1.0 [T12].

## 2. Classification and assessment that change the plan

- **Doha entities** (history plus examination): adductor-related (adductor tenderness and pain on resisted adduction), iliopsoas-related (tenderness and pain on resisted hip flexion or stretch), inguinal-related (inguinal canal tenderness, no palpable hernia, worse with Valsalva/cough), pubic-related (symphysis tenderness, no particular resistance test); hip-related (FADIR, imaging); other [T1, T14]. Entities coexist; King's cohort was 64% pubic aponeurosis, 17% adductor, 15% hip, 4% iliopsoas [T3].
- **Tests that differentiate** (strong evidence): adductor squeeze pain and force, hip internal rotation, bent-knee fall-out, trunk muscle function, PROMs (HAGOS, iHOT) [T24]. External rotation does not differ from controls [T24].
- **Acute adductor injury**: three adductor tests predict MRI lesion and location (PPV 93–97%); palpation absence rules out (NPV 91–96%) [T22] — see `muscle-strains.md` for the acute program.
- **Hip-related pain**: FAI syndrome = symptoms + signs + cam/pincer morphology; dysplasia/instability and labral or chondral lesions are the other common conditions; exclude non-musculoskeletal and serious causes first [T4, T21].
- **Norms**: adult male footballers (hand-held dynamometry, kg-force) adduction 35.6 kg at 0° hip flexion, 32.0 at 45°, 25.5 at 90°; hip flexion 47 kg [T18]. Female national-team players (eccentric, side-lying) adduction 122 N (2.3 Nm/kg) at 13 years to 170 N (2.5 Nm/kg) at 20+, ratio ~1.0 ± 0.2 [T12]. Squeeze test values in athletes with groin pain rose from 81/159/122 mmHg (0°/45°/90°) at entry to 135/234/209 at discharge [T3].

## 3. Stages and criteria (long-standing groin pain)

| Stage | Goal | Content | Progress when | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Control and strength | Settle pain; intersegmental (lumbopelvic–hip) control; strength | Gluteal and iliopsoas activation, trunk control, compound lifts (deadlift, squat, lunge); adductor loading introduced as tolerated (isometric squeeze → side-lying adduction → short-lever Copenhagen) | Technique competent; squeeze pain settling; daily tasks pain-free | 3–5 weeks [T3, T9, T23] |
| 2 Linear running | Load tolerance in running; fix overstride and anterior pelvic tilt | Graded running volume and speed; long-lever Copenhagen; hip flexor and abductor strength | Running pain-free at building speed; squeeze pain-free at 0° and 45° | 2–3 weeks [T3] |
| 3 Multidirectional | Rate of force development in three planes; cutting, reactive agility, sprint transitions | Change-of-direction drills, kicking, sport-specific skills; full Copenhagen 12–15 reps | Pain-free squeeze at 0°, 45° and 90°; pain-free cutting and sprinting; full training session completed | 2–3 weeks [T3] |
| Return and maintenance | Back in sport without recurrence | Copenhagen 1 × 12–15 per side once a week; running and change-of-direction exposure kept in training | Monitor with the 5-second squeeze test through the season | ongoing [T9, T20] |

Mean time to pain-free return 9.9 ± 3.5 weeks with ~5 visits; 73% returned, 27% not followed, so treat 73% as a ceiling [T3]. Hölmich's active program (8–12 weeks: static and dynamic adductor strengthening, coordination and trunk work, progressing to sport-specific loading) returned 68% versus 12% [T23].

## 4. Hip-related pain / FAI syndrome

- **Program** *(consensus and trial arms)*: hip abductor/extensor/adductor strengthening, trunk stability, neuromuscular and movement control, education and activity modification (avoid provocative deep flexion–adduction–internal rotation early), then functional and sport-specific loading; progression by volume, load and complexity (isolated → compound → single-leg → unstable) [T2, T6, T8, T17].
- **Duration**: at least 3 months; programs ≥3 months did more than 3–7-week programs in the underlying review [T2]. Trial physiotherapy arms (6–12 sessions over 6 weeks to 5–6 months) were shorter [T5, T6, T17].
- **Return to sport** *(consensus)*: strength, cardiovascular fitness, basic athletic movements and load tolerance first; then running, high speed, cutting and provocative positions at speed; monitor with HAGOS/iHOT and physical measures [T2].
- **Surgery**: an accepted option alongside rehabilitation [T4]; better than short physiotherapy at 8–12 months in three RCTs, with no difference at 6 months in one pooled analysis and no difference in later arthroplasty [T5–T7, T17]. Shared decision after a proper trial of exercise *(inference from T2 and T26)*.
- Injections, medication, manual therapy, taping, bracing and orthotics: insufficient evidence; research priorities only [T2].

## 5. Dosage

| Program | Dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen adduction (prevention) | 1 set per side: week 1 2×/week × 3–5 reps; week 2 3×/week × 3–5; weeks 3–4 3×/week × 7–10; weeks 5–6 3×/week × 12–15; weeks 7–8 2×/week × 12–15; in season 1×/week × 12–15 (<5 min); levels: side-lying adduction → short lever → full; step down if pain >3/10 | T9 |
| Copenhagen (strength trials) | 8 weeks, 2–3 sessions/week, 1–4 sets × 3–15 reps (9–90 reps/week); eccentric adduction +0.61 Nm/kg; compliance 85–91%, soreness 0–2/10 | T10, T25 |
| Copenhagen (rehab, review) | 2–3 sessions/week, ~180–320 reps/week over 8 weeks; strength and symptom gains, few sole-intervention trials | T11 |
| Criterion-based groin program | 3 levels by goals, not calendar; ~5 clinic visits; sets/reps not reported — use `training-variables.md` (strength 3–4 × 6–12 at 1–3 RIR; control/endurance 2–3 × 12–20) | T3 |
| Active training (Hölmich) | 8–12 weeks, static and dynamic adductor and trunk strengthening, coordination, progressing to sport-specific work | T23 |
| Hip-related pain | ≥3 months; 5–24 supervised sessions in trials; daily to 3×/week; progression by volume, load, complexity | T2, T8 |

## 6. Pain and load rules

- Copenhagen levels: regress a level if groin pain exceeds 3/10 during the exercise [T9]; expected soreness 0–2/10 [T25].
- Criterion-based progression uses technique competence plus symptom level; running and cutting pain-free before return; pain-free squeeze at three angles before discharge [T3].
- Keep training where possible: entities are chronic (median 32 weeks) and respond to loading, not rest [T3, T23].
- Hip-related pain: avoid provocative impingement positions early, then reintroduce controlled range at speed late [T2, T6].

## 7. Return to sport and outcomes

- Groin pain: pain-free squeeze at 0°, 45° and 90°, pain-free running and cutting, full training; HAGOS Sport 51 → 86 and Symptoms 60 → 89 at discharge [T3]. Full return without groin pain is the Hölmich outcome (68% vs 12%) [T23].
- Hip-related pain: staged return *(consensus)* [T2]; iHOT-33 MCID 6.1; FASHIoN 12-month iHOT-33 difference +6.8 for arthroscopy [T5]; FAIT 8-month HOS-ADL +10 [T17].
- Prevalence context: 14% point prevalence in elite female team sport (volleyball 31%, ice hockey 20%) [T19].

## 8. Prevention

- Copenhagen adduction program: OR 0.59 for any groin problem (13.5% vs 21.3% weekly prevalence), OR 0.82 (NS) for substantial problems; adherence 73% pre-season and 70% in season [T9].
- Strength gain is the mechanism (eccentric adduction +0.61 Nm/kg in youth; +36% in one trial) [T10, T11, T25]; a 2025 meta-analysis on the exercise is retracted [T11].
- Programs that add the Copenhagen exercise to FIFA 11+ supply the eccentric adduction stimulus the 11+ lacks *(commentary)* [T20]; exercise-based prevention trials mostly use the Copenhagen exercise (13 of 19) and rarely report injury incidence; combine with multifaceted programs [T13].
- Risk screening does not identify at-risk players reliably; favour global neuromuscular training plus load-error avoidance [T20].

## 9. Red lines

- Exclude serious and competing causes before loading: stress fracture, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, tumour, infection, inguinal hernia, lumbar referral [T21] and refer anything atypical.
- Do not treat MRI findings; match them to the clinical entity [T27].
- No isolated adductor loading beyond pain 3/10; no cutting before pain-free running and squeeze [T3, T9].
- Hip-related pain not improving after a proper ≥3-month program: discuss surgical opinion [T2, T5].

## 10. Evidence confidence and gaps

- Strong: Doha classification and the examination tests; active training over passive care (one older RCT); Copenhagen prevention (one large cluster RCT); arthroscopy vs short physiotherapy at 8–12 months.
- Moderate: the criterion-based program (uncontrolled cohort of 205); Copenhagen strength gains (youth trials, low certainty).
- Weak or missing: entity-specific exercise evidence for iliopsoas-, inguinal- and pubic-related pain; sets/reps/loads for groin rehab; return-to-sport criteria beyond the squeeze test; female and adolescent rehab data; risk-factor data for Australian football and hockey; physiotherapy programs of adequate dose compared with arthroscopy.
- A 2025 Scandinavian Copenhagen meta-analysis is retracted; a 2021 meta-analysis reportedly finding surgery not superior for FAI was seen only as a title.
