# BASE Health Noosa — exercise programming guide

**What this is.** The reference Claude follows when it suggests an exercise program from a testing report ("Suggest from the report" in the program builder): a Performance & Readiness screen, a Lower-Limb Strength report or, from v24, a Hamstring or ACL rehab report. It covers how to pick exercises for each finding, how many to pick, the order of a session, and the training variables (sets, reps, load, rest, tempo, intent). The physiotherapist checks and edits every suggestion; this guide shapes the first draft.

**Status: a first draft written from published evidence (sources at the end) for the clinic to review and edit.** Where BASE does things differently, change this file: the app reads it the next time it opens online. Keep each rule short and concrete; Claude follows what is written here over its general knowledge.

---

## 1. From a finding to a training quality

Work from the report's priorities: Off target first, then Close. Each finding points to a training quality and a family of exercises. Pick the best fit for the person and the setting, not the first on the list.

**Train the quality, not the test (v28).** The test result is the evidence and the re-test; the aim of the program is the physical quality behind it. Write "increase lower-body power output", not "improve jump height"; "maximal strength", not "the IMTP number"; "reactive strength", not "RSI"; "eccentric hamstring strength", not "the Nordic score"; "acceleration", not "the 10 m time". The same goes for titles, day headings and the why line on each exercise ("Power output: CMJ peak power 42 W/kg, target ≥ 48").

**The DSI is not a finding (v28).** It has no target and is never good or bad, so it is never a priority or a deficit. It says how the athlete uses their force, and sets the emphasis and the training variables of the power and strength work (section 4), read with the IMTP and CMJ values.

### Performance & Readiness screen (ForceDecks, ForceFrame, NordBord, sprint)

| Finding | What it usually means | Quality to train | Exercise families (best fit first) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jump height or peak power low **and** IMTP relative force low | The engine is small: not enough force to be fast | Maximal strength first, power second | Heavy multi-joint lower-body strength (trap-bar deadlift, back or front squat, leg press); add one vertical power exercise at low volume |
| Jump height or peak power low **but** IMTP relative force on target (the DSI is then usually below 0.60) | Strong but not yet turning it into power output | Power output: ballistic work | Squat jump, box jump, countermovement jump variations, loaded jumps (light), Olympic-lift derivatives if coached |
| DSI below about 0.60 *(sets the emphasis; not a finding)* | High maximal force; a small share of it used in the jump | Emphasis on ballistic and plyometric work | Jumps and plyometrics with maximal intent; keep heavy strength to one maintenance exercise |
| DSI 0.60–0.80 *(sets the emphasis; not a finding)* | Force used in the jump in proportion to maximal force | Both | One strength and one power exercise |
| DSI above about 0.80 *(sets the emphasis; not a finding)* | Most of the maximal force already used in the jump | Emphasis on maximal strength | Heavy multi-joint strength as above; keep one ballistic exercise |
| RSI-modified low (slow, long contact) | Poor fast stretch-shortening (stiffness) | Fast-SSC plyometrics | Pogo hops, hurdle hops, drop jumps from a low box (progress height only when ground contact stays short), ankle stiffness drills |
| Concentric or landing asymmetry > 10% | One leg doing less (or landing harder) | Unilateral strength and control on the weaker side | Split squat / rear-foot-elevated split squat, single-leg press, single-leg landings and hops with a quiet landing; put the weaker side first and add a set on it |
| IMTP peak force or relative force low | Low maximal / isometric force | Maximal strength, isometric strength | Heavy multi-joint strength as above; isometric mid-thigh pull holds or heavy isometric squat holds; progress to heavier strength work |
| Nordic peak force low (either side) | Weak eccentric hamstrings | Eccentric hamstring strength, plus hip-dominant hamstring strength | Nordic hamstring curl (knee-dominant, eccentric) **and** one hip-dominant exercise (Romanian deadlift, 45° hip extension, single-leg RDL); these target different hamstring muscles, so one of each is not a duplicate |
| Nordic L/R imbalance above target | One hamstring weaker | As above, biased to the weaker side | Single-leg eccentric work (single-leg RDL, single-leg hamstring slider) on the weaker side; Nordics with the weaker side emphasised (lean toward it, or an extra set) |
| Adductor peak force low, or adduction:abduction ratio outside the band | Groin strength deficit or imbalance | Eccentric adductor strength (and abductor if that is the low side) | Copenhagen adduction (progress short lever → long lever), side-lying adduction, adductor squeeze isometrics; for abductors: side-lying hip abduction, banded lateral walks, single-leg squat with level pelvis |
| Abductor peak force low | Weak hip abductors / lateral hip | Abductor strength and control | Side-lying hip abduction, banded lateral walks, single-leg Romanian deadlift, lateral step-down |
| 10 m sprint slow | Poor acceleration / horizontal force | Horizontal force and power | Resisted sled sprints or pushes, hip-dominant hamstring strength (RDL, hip thrust), broad jumps and bounding, acceleration drills (wall drills, falling starts) |
| 20 m sprint slow with 10 m on target | Poor top-speed transition | Fast-SSC plyometrics and sprint exposure | Bounding, flying sprints (if space), hurdle hops, hamstring strength |

### Lower-Limb Strength & Capacity battery

| Test low | Quality | Exercise families |
|---|---|---|
| Split squat (per leg) | Unilateral knee-dominant strength | Split squat → rear-foot-elevated split squat → single-leg press; progress load before complexity |
| Seated calf raise (soleus, bent knee) | Soleus strength | Seated calf raise (heavy), bent-knee calf raise on a step |
| Standing / Smith calf raise (gastrocnemius) | Gastrocnemius strength | Straight-knee single-leg calf raise with load, standing calf raise |
| Single-leg calf raise reps (below 25) | Calf endurance / capacity | Single-leg calf raise to a rhythm, 2–3 sets to near-failure, then add load |
| Single-leg knee extension | Quadriceps strength | Knee extension (single leg), leg press, split squat |
| Single-leg bridge reps | Hip extension endurance | Single-leg bridge / hip thrust, progressing to loaded hip thrust |
| Prone hamstring curl | Knee-flexor strength | Prone or seated hamstring curl, Nordic hamstring curl (eccentric), hamstring slider |
| Hip abduction / adduction, or the ratio | As for the screen | As for the screen (Copenhagen, side-lying abduction, banded walks) |

## 2. One exercise per training quality

Two exercises that train the same quality in the same way add fatigue, not benefit. For example, a box jump and a squat jump are both vertical ballistic jumps with the same outcome: pick one. Choose the exercise that best matches the finding, the person's experience and the setting.

Families (pick at most one from each per session, unless a second is an easier version used as a warm-up set):

- **Vertical power:** squat jump, box jump, countermovement jump, hurdle jump (two-foot), loaded jump.
- **Horizontal power:** broad jump, bounding, single-leg broad jump.
- **Fast stretch-shortening (stiffness):** pogo hops, hurdle hops, drop jump, ankle hops.
- **Knee-dominant strength:** back squat, front squat, goblet squat, leg press, split squat, rear-foot-elevated split squat, step-up, single-leg squat.
- **Hip-dominant strength:** trap-bar or conventional deadlift, Romanian deadlift, single-leg RDL, hip thrust, 45° hip extension.
- **Eccentric hamstring (knee-dominant):** Nordic hamstring curl, hamstring slider / curl with slow lowering.
- **Hamstring curl (knee flexion):** prone, seated or Swiss-ball hamstring curl.
- **Adductor:** Copenhagen adduction, side-lying adduction, adductor squeeze.
- **Abductor / lateral hip:** side-lying hip abduction, banded lateral walk, lateral step-down, single-leg RDL (control).
- **Calf, straight knee:** standing or single-leg calf raise, Smith calf raise.
- **Calf, bent knee:** seated calf raise, bent-knee calf raise.
- **Isometric holds:** isometric mid-thigh pull, isometric squat hold, wall sit, isometric calf hold, adductor squeeze hold.
- **Landing / control:** single-leg landings, lateral hops with stick, step-down with level pelvis.

Note the exceptions written into section 1: a Nordic (knee-dominant eccentric) plus a Romanian deadlift (hip-dominant) is *two* qualities, not a duplicate; a straight-knee calf raise plus a seated calf raise target different calf muscles.

**How many:** 4–8 exercises in all, usually 5–6. Cover the top two or three priorities properly rather than every finding thinly. One maintenance exercise for a clear strength is enough.

## 3. Order of a session

1. **Fast and powerful first**, while fresh: plyometrics, jumps, sprints and sled work (quality, not fatigue).
2. **Heaviest strength next:** the main multi-joint lift (squat, deadlift, leg press).
3. **Unilateral and assistance strength:** split squat, hip thrust, calf raises, hamstring curls.
4. **Eccentric and isometric work:** Nordics, Copenhagen, isometric holds (these can be placed earlier when they are the main priority and the person is used to them).
5. **Capacity, control and mobility last:** high-rep calf raises, single-leg bridges, landings, mobility.

The exercise for the **main priority goes early** in the session, where it will be done best. Sections on the handout should follow this order (for example "Power", "Strength", "Hamstrings and hips", "Calf and control"), with a heading that says the intent of the block.

## 4. Training variables by intent

Give every exercise sets, reps, a load guide, rest, tempo where it matters, and an intent cue in the notes. "RIR" is reps in reserve: how many more reps could have been done.

| Intent | Sets × reps | Load guide | Rest | Tempo | Cue for the notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Maximal strength** | 3–5 × 3–6 | Heavy: about 85% of 1RM, or 2 RIR; stop the set when bar speed drops clearly (about 10–20% slower) rather than grinding | 2–3 min (up to 5 min on the biggest lifts); 1–2 min is enough for someone new to training | Controlled down (2 s), drive up fast | "Every rep as fast as you can on the way up" |
| **Power / ballistic** (jumps, throws, Olympic-lift derivatives) | 3–5 × 3–6 | Body weight for jumps; loaded jumps about 30% of 1RM; Olympic-lift derivatives and other loaded power work 30–70% of 1RM; stop the set when speed drops (about 10–20% slower) | 2–3 min, full recovery | Explosive; reset between reps | "Maximal effort every rep; stop the set if it slows" |
| **Plyometric, fast-SSC** (pogos, hurdle hops, drop jumps) | 3–5 × 5–8 contacts; about 60–100 ground contacts per session for a beginner, up to 120–140 for the well trained | Body weight | 90 s – 2 min between sets (work:rest about 1:5 to 1:10) | Shortest ground contact possible | "Stiff ankle, short contact, land quietly" |
| **Hypertrophy / strength capacity** | 3–4 × 8–12; for a trained person aim for 10–20 hard sets per muscle group across the week | Moderate: 2–3 RIR | 60–90 s | 2 s down, 1–2 s up | "Smooth and controlled, 2–3 reps in reserve" |
| **Local endurance / capacity** (calf raise reps, bridges) | 2–3 × 15–25+ | Body weight to light | 45–60 s | Rhythmic | "Steady rhythm, full range, stop 2 reps short of failure" |
| **Eccentric hamstring (Nordic)** | Start 2 × 5, build to 3 × 8–12 over 6–10 weeks | Body weight (partner or strap) | 2 min | Lower over 3–4 s, push back up | "Lower as slowly as you can; keep hips straight" |
| **Eccentric emphasis (other)** | 3 × 6–8 | Moderate | 90 s – 2 min | 3–4 s lowering, normal lift | "Slow on the way down" |
| **Copenhagen adduction** | One set per side, 2–3 times a week: start 3–5 reps, build to 12–15 over 8 weeks (a second set is optional); short lever before long lever; then one set a week to keep it | Body weight | 60–90 s | Controlled | "Keep the body in a straight line; lower under control" |
| **Heavy slow resistance (tendon loading)** | 3–4 × 15 reps in weeks 1–3, to 4 × 6 by week 12 | 15RM progressing to 6RM | 2–3 min | 3 s up, 3 s down | "Slow and heavy both ways" |
| **Isometric hold** (tendon pain, or isometric strength) | 5 × 30–45 s | About 70% of maximum, heavy | 2 min | Hold still | "Hold hard and still; breathe" |
| **Isometric for force / rate of force** (IMTP holds, isometric squat) | 3–5 × 3–5 s maximal | Maximal effort against an immovable bar | 2–3 min | Build to maximum fast | "Pull as hard and fast as you can" |
| **Mobility / control** | 2–3 × 8–12, or 30–60 s holds | Body weight | Minimal | Slow | "Only as far as you can control" |

**The DSI sets the emphasis (v28).** The DSI is not rated, so it never asks for an exercise of its own; it changes the variables of the power and strength work. Below about 0.60 (with a good IMTP): the power rows lead (body weight to about 30% of 1RM moved as fast as possible, 3–6 reps, full rest) and one heavy lift is kept at 3 × 3–5. Between 0.60 and 0.80: one maximal strength and one power exercise at the table's doses. Above about 0.80 (with a modest IMTP): the maximal strength row leads (about 85% of 1RM, 3–6 reps, 2–3 min rest) and one ballistic exercise is kept. A DSI moves when either the IMTP or the CMJ force changes, so read it with both numbers (performance-tests guide).

**Experience.** New to training: 1–3 sets of 8–12 at a moderate load (3–4 RIR), 2–3 sessions a week, technique first; no drop jumps, heavy eccentrics or maximal isometrics until the basics are solid; progress load by 2–10% once 1–2 extra reps come easily. Trained: use the table as written. Adolescents: body-weight and technique progressions first, beginner plyometric volumes.

**Load as a guide, not a number.** Write loads the person can act on: "body weight", "a backpack or dumbbells", "heavy, 2 reps in reserve", "a weight you could lift 8 times". The physiotherapist sets any exact kilograms.

**Side.** Unilateral exercises are "Each side"; for an asymmetry, say which side gets the extra set in the notes.

## 5. Weekly structure

- **2 sessions a week:** full-body both days; the main priority in both; power at low volume both days; one heavier strength day and one moderate.
- **3 sessions a week:** power plus main strength (day 1), strength plus hamstrings and hips (day 2), capacity and control plus a light power touch (day 3).
- **4 sessions a week:** two lower-body days at least 48 hours apart plus two lighter days (capacity, control, mobility, or upper body if the person wants it); plyometrics on 2–3 days at most, 48–72 hours apart.
- Each muscle group or quality twice a week beats once; the main lifts are better spread over the week than crammed into one day.
- **Time-poor:** one or two hard sets of 6–12 reps per main lift, two or three days a week, maintains and still improves strength.

The program is written out by training day: one section per day with a heading that says what the day is for ("Day 1: Power output and maximal strength", "Day 2: Strength and hamstrings", "Day 3: Capacity and control"), 3–5 exercises a day. The same exercise may appear on two days with a heavier and a lighter load; never two exercises for the same quality on one day. Someone new to training can repeat the same two or three full-body sessions. The handout's general instructions should say the sessions per week and the block length in one line (for example "3 sessions a week for 6 weeks, at least a day between sessions").

## 6. Setting

- **Gym:** barbells, trap bar, leg press, cables, boxes and hurdles are available; use them for the heavy and ballistic work.
- **Home:** body weight, bands, a backpack or dumbbells, a step; Nordics with a strap under a couch or a partner; single-leg versions to add load; plyometrics only on a safe, non-slip surface; isometrics against a wall, a strap or a doorframe.
- **Both:** heavy and ballistic work on gym days, capacity and control at home.

---

## 7. Block length and progression

- **4 weeks:** one wave: learn in week 1, load weeks 2–3, lighter week 4 before retesting.
- **6 weeks:** load weeks 1–3 (add a set or 2–10% load each week once technique is clean), ease in week 4, load weeks 5–6, retest.
- **8 weeks:** two 4-week waves; progress the exercise (for example split squat → rear-foot-elevated split squat; box jump → hurdle jump) in the second wave if the first went well.
- Progress one thing at a time: load, then reps or sets, then exercise difficulty.
- Retest at the end of the block, matching the "next retest" date on the report where one is set.

## 8. One focus per block (v26)

A block is not a little of everything. Each block has one focus, chosen in this order and named in the program's title:

1. **An injury or condition that explains the flagged results** (same side, a quality it is known to lower at this stage) leads the block; its loading goes first on its days (section 9).
2. **Otherwise the largest deficit in the quality the sport needs most.** Low maximal strength (IMTP relative force, quad ISO, the main lifts) → maximal strength first, with one ballistic exercise kept; maximal strength good but power output (CMJ power and jump) or reactive strength (RSI) low → ballistic and reactive power, with one heavy lift kept; a one-sided deficit with a consistent direction → the weaker side's quality. The DSI is never the focus or a deficit: it only sets the emphasis (section 4).
3. **At most one secondary quality**, and one heavy exercise that keeps up a clear strength if there is room.
4. **Everything else is deferred**, by name, to a later block, in the order strength (or rehab loading) → power and speed → sport-specific; the rationale lists what was deferred and what re-test opens the next block.

The focus decides what comes first in the session, what gets the most sets and what is progressed; it does not ban the other qualities. The physiotherapist's notes in the Suggest dialog (v27: the sport, the equipment, what to avoid, what the block is for) are followed ahead of these defaults, within section 10's red lines, and the rationale says how. The evidence guide "Designing the block" carries the detail: the single-focus block is best supported for power, speed-strength and jump, and for sequencing strength before power; for maximal strength and size, undulating and mixed sessions do as well.

## 9. One program for a condition and the performance findings (v25)

When a condition is given with the request (chosen in the Suggest dialog with its side and stage, or fixed by a Hamstring or ACL report with its injured side and phase), the report is read as a whole before any exercise is chosen. The evidence guide "Rehab and performance in one program" carries the detail; BASE's rules are:

1. **Sort the flagged findings first.** Explained by the condition: same side or region, and a quality that condition is known to lower at this stage (quadriceps and jump symmetry after ACL reconstruction; calf strength, hop reactive strength and RFD with Achilles tendinopathy; eccentric knee-flexor strength after a hamstring strain; knee-extensor strength with patellar tendinopathy or patellofemoral pain; adductor strength with groin pain; balance and eversion strength after an ankle sprain). Separate: the other side, another region, or a quality the condition does not touch. Keep: a clear strength the sport needs.
2. **Each day's heading says whether it is rehab-led or performance-led** ("Day 1: Rehab: Achilles loading, plus strength"; "Day 2: Performance: jump power"; "Day 3: Capacity and keep-up"): the condition's loading from the stage's rows, the separate deficits within the stage's pain and load rules for the affected tissue, and a strength kept with one or two heavy exercises. A deficit the condition explains is not given a second exercise as performance work.
3. **Say what leads.** Early and middle stages: rehab leads; the performance section stays small, trains the other side and the untouched qualities, and avoids the injured tissue's high-strain loads (plyometrics, sprints, cutting). Late: the two merge; the condition's energy-storage, running and change-of-direction work doubles as the performance block. Ongoing: performance leads with a maintenance dose for the condition. Maximal strength before power output when maximal strength is low; ballistic and reactive work when maximal strength is good and power output or reactive strength is low (the DSI only sets the emphasis, section 4).
4. **Session order stays as section 3** on each day: fast first, heavy next, the condition's eccentric and isometric work after the strength lifts unless it is the main priority, capacity last.
5. **Progression inside the block goes on the exercise** ("weeks 1–3 double leg, from week 4 single leg"), and the instructions line agrees with it.
6. **A rationale for the physiotherapist** comes with every suggestion (kept with the program, never printed): what leads and why, which findings were treated as the condition and which as separate, what was held back because of the stage, what the next block adds and the sign that opens it (a 24-hour pain level, a symmetry, a test number, a time floor), and what to re-test. The physiotherapist edits or clears it.
7. **Never infer** a condition, a side or a stage from the numbers; without a condition there is no rehab section. Where the side is not given, a one-sided deficit is left unresolved and the rationale says so.

## 10. Red lines

- Never a diagnosis, an injury prediction or a return-to-sport decision: the physiotherapist owns those.
- No maximal plyometrics, drop jumps, heavy eccentrics or maximal isometrics for someone new to training, or where the report's notes mention pain; start with the easier version of the family.
- A change the report marks as "within normal variation" is not a change; don't program for it.
- Hamstring and ACL rehab programs (and any program with a condition chosen) also follow the evidence guides sent with the request (rehab principles and the condition's guide): the physiotherapist's rehab phase or stage decides which rows of their stage tables apply, and nothing from a later stage is programmed. Where this guide and an evidence guide differ, this guide wins.
- When the library has nothing suitable and nothing better is clearly justified, say so in the notes rather than inventing something elaborate.
